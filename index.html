      📖 Trading glossary — how to read all of this

      — and on the phone, tap any badge or column header for its explanation

      Patient vs instant offers. The GE matches by price. Offer above the going rate and you buy instantly at the best available price; offer at or below it and you join a queue and wait. Every flip here is two patient offers: buy at the low, sell at the high. Instant trades are only for margin tests and emergency exits — they pay the spread instead of earning it.

      Buy / Sell columns. "Buy" is what patient buyers are currently paying (the wiki's low / "instasell"); "Sell" is what patient sellers are receiving (the high / "instabuy"). The gap between them, minus tax, is the whole business.

      Margin, ROI. Margin = sell − buy − tax, per unit, for one full round trip. ROI is that margin divided by the buy price: the return on the gp you tie up. 1–2% per cycle compounds fast because a cycle is hours, not months.

      Margin test. Live prices can be two unrelated trades. To learn the real spread: instant-buy one unit (offer ~5% above the shown sell — the fill is the true sell side), instant-sell it back (~5% below the shown buy — the fill is the true buy side). The unit loses the spread + tax; that's the fee for real numbers. Either offer sitting over ~5 minutes is a fail in itself. Tests expire after 16h.

      Buy limit. Each item caps how much you can buy per rolling 4-hour window (e.g. 25,000 runes). The window rolls while your offer stands, so an offer sitting all day buys several windows' worth.

      Slots. You get 8 GE offer slots. The plan commits 7 so one is always free for buying gear or supplies without cancelling a flip. An idle committed slot earns exactly zero.

      One-third rule. Never put more than a third of your stack into one item — one bad market can't take out the whole bankroll, and smaller positions exit faster.

      Thin-side volume. An item trades on two sides (people buying, people selling). Your fills move at the pace of the quieter side, so all sizing and gating here uses that number, not the total.

      Crowded. Top-100 by 24h volume means every flipping tool recommends it, and competition has squeezed the spread to the tax floor. Volume there is safety, not profit — the edge lives in the mid-liquidity items nobody's tool ranks.

      Trend / catching a knife. A wide margin on a falling chart usually means the price is on its way through your buy price. Flat charts are what you want; the plan benches anything drifting worse than −5% over 7 days.

      Caution: seeds & pump vehicles. Cheap, high-volume, storable items (seeds, impling jars, ensouled heads, nests) are the classic targets for price manipulation. They flip great — until the pump ends. The plan half-sizes them and allows one slot per category until your own log proves an item out (3 profitable flips in 14 days).

      Thesis clusters. Items that move on one story — wilderness gear, raid supplies, clue rewards — are a single bet wearing several names, and the family rule can't see it (different production lines, same patch note). Tag them yourself, or confirm what the weekly co-movement scan (daily returns net of the all-item average), shared rare wiki categories, and the built-in seed list propose. A confirmed cluster's combined exposure is capped at a share of working capital by the allocator, its P&L is reported per cluster in the review, and the Wednesday update-day advisory names its members. Candidates — suspected, dormant, or seeded — never cap anything until you ratify them.

      Undercut to exit. A sell that won't fill: relist it 1 gp under the current low sell price and take the small loss. Hoping is how a 2% edge becomes a 10% hole.

      Capture rate & horizons. Sizing assumes a standing offer captures ~15% of the item's flow — you're one buyer among many, not the whole market. Buy legs are sized to fill inside the fill horizon (default 4h); buy limits keep rolling every 4h across the full day horizon while offers stand. The reserve is gp the plan never touches.

      Tax-multiple rule. Margin must clear 3× the per-unit tax and 15 ticks. When margin ≈ tax, even a scratch exit (selling at your own buy price) loses the entire tax — the Tarromin shape. At 3×, giving back two-thirds of the edge still exits positive.

      Ticks. Prices move in 1 gp steps, so a 4 gp margin is four ticks of room: one tick of adverse move erases a quarter of the edge. This is why cheap items lose with healthy-looking ROI % — the floor here is 15 ticks and the scanner defaults to 400+ gp items.

      Adverse selection (stale bids). A bid that fills hours after placing usually fills because the price moved down through it, not because patience paid. That's why unfilled buys are flagged "cancel — market moved" after the fill horizon, and why late fills aren't a win.

      gp / slot-hour. Profit divided by the hours a flip actually occupied a slot — the truest measure of what deserves your 7 slots, and what the plan learns from your log.

      What's this item even for? Hover or tap any item's name for what it ultimately becomes in-game and why it has the daily volume it does — consistent volume always has a consumption engine behind it (XP grinds, PvM burn, teleports, prayer). The ↗ next to watchlist names opens the wiki page.

      Tax rules implemented

      2% of the sale price, deducted when the sell fills, rounded down per item, capped at 5,000,000 gp per item. Rate rose from 1% to 2% on 29 May 2025. Items selling below 50 gp owe nothing, and 48 items are explicitly exempt, tagged no tax. Verified against the OSRS Wiki. Data: RuneLite / OSRS Wiki real-time prices — polled no faster than 60s, with backoff when the API is unhappy.
